Treatment methods

Physiotherapy

A solid general understanding of the musculoskeletal system is the most important factor in being able to distinguish between different complaint patterns. Is a complaint local, or can it be traced back to the back or neck?

Read more >

McKenzie therapy

A “poor” posture that has been present for so many years isn't easy to change. Postural advice is important here, but also very hard to keep up. McKenzie therapy works with “repeated movements” – performing a specific (brief) exercise very frequently throughout the day. A highly effective method for reducing posture-related complaints.

Read more >

Watson Headache Method

According to Dean Watson, most headaches originate in the neck. He focuses mainly on how the top three vertebrae move in relation to one another. From there, the headache can be triggered or reduced.

Read more >

Dry Needling

Dry needling is a method based on trigger points – specific points in a muscle where tension tends to build up and stay. Inserting an acupuncture needle into these points triggers a reflex that allows the muscle to quickly relax again.

My approach

In my view, modern society spends far too much time bent over and far too little time stretched out!

Laptop work, long stretches looking at an iPad or phone, Netflix on the sofa, and even reading a book or knitting – all activities that put a real strain on the neck and lower back. It may not sound like much at first, but a day of “doing nothing” is already something the neck and lower back don't appreciate.

This often results in complaints originating in the neck, such as headaches, neck pain, shoulder complaints, dizziness or tinnitus. From the back, this can radiate into the buttock, hip, groin or further down the leg, all the way to the toes.
